There is no universally agreed answer because it depends on which rights and moral principles one prioritizes.

- **Those who support bans** argue that the fetus has a right to life and that abortion is morally comparable to ending an innocent human life, even when the pregnancy does not threaten the woman’s life. They may support exceptions for rape, incest, or severe fetal abnormalities.
- **Those who oppose bans** argue that pregnancy requires a person to use their body for another’s benefit, and the government should not compel that use. They emphasize bodily autonomy, the physical and psychological burdens of pregnancy, and the difficulty of enforcing exceptions fairly.
- **Intermediate positions** support legal abortion early in pregnancy but allow more restrictions later, usually with exceptions for serious health risks, rape, incest, or nonviable fetal conditions.

A practical complication is that “the woman’s life is not at risk” is not always a clear medical category: serious health risks can develop rapidly, and doctors may hesitate to intervene if laws threaten criminal penalties. Any policy also has to address rape, minors, severe fetal abnormalities, and pregnancies that endanger health without posing an immediate risk of death.

So, whether it *should* be illegal is ultimately a value judgment. A blanket ban prioritizes fetal legal protection over bodily autonomy; allowing abortion prioritizes the pregnant person’s autonomy and medical decision-making.
