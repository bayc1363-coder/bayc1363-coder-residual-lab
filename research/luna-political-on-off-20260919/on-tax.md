## Frame A — Progressive-capacity frame

Rich people are taxed appropriately when taxation follows ability to pay and funds conditions that sustain shared prosperity. High incomes and large fortunes arise within publicly maintained systems—law, infrastructure, educated workers, financial stability, and property rights. Progressive taxes then finance those systems, reduce extreme concentration, and expand access to opportunity. The resulting redistribution can strengthen social mobility and democratic legitimacy, especially when the tax base includes capital gains, inheritances, and other forms of wealth that otherwise escape ordinary wage taxation.

On this frame, the rich are taxed too little when they can convert labor income into lightly taxed capital income, defer gains indefinitely, use deductions unavailable to ordinary workers, or pass large fortunes across generations with limited tax. The key comparison is not whether their nominal tax bill is large, but whether their effective rate reflects their economic capacity and the public institutions that helped produce their wealth.

## Frame B — Incentive-and-limits frame

Rich people are taxed too highly when additional taxation weakens the productive activity that generates investment, innovation, employment, and future revenue. High marginal rates and taxes on capital can encourage avoidance, relocation, reduced risk-taking, or the substitution of less productive tax strategies for enterprise. Those responses can shrink the tax base, slow growth, reduce wages or investment, and shift the eventual burden toward workers and consumers. A tax system therefore remains legitimate when it raises necessary revenue while preserving strong incentives to create, invest, and keep economic activity within the jurisdiction.

On this frame, the rich are taxed too highly when governments keep increasing rates after revenue gains flatten, when taxes penalize unrealized or mobile wealth, or when complex rules reward lobbying and avoidance. The central test is not whether affluent people can pay more, but whether the extra revenue exceeds the economic and administrative costs created by the tax.

## Residual

The answer depends on the tax mix, effective rather than headline rates, enforcement, mobility, public spending quality, and the measured behavioral response; these facts vary substantially by country and period.

## Working answer

The rich are taxed too highly in some systems and too lightly in others: the strongest judgment depends on whether additional progressive taxation produces durable public benefits and fairer opportunity, or instead produces large avoidance and investment costs that reduce the tax base and shift burdens downward.
