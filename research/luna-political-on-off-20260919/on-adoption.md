## Frame A — Equal parental eligibility

1. Adoption should allocate children to safe, capable, committed homes.  
2. Sexual orientation does not itself establish a parenting defect or an inability to provide stability, care, or attachment.  
3. Therefore, excluding same-sex couples treats a non-relevant characteristic as disqualifying.  
4. The fair rule is to evaluate couples by the same criteria: safety, permanence, emotional capacity, resources, and suitability for the particular child.

## Frame B — Child-centered precaution

1. Adoption exists to secure a child’s welfare, not to vindicate an adult’s entitlement to parent.  
2. A responsible system should therefore examine evidence about stability, caregiving quality, family support, stigma, and the child’s specific needs.  
3. Concerns about family structure warrant scrutiny, but they do not justify a categorical exclusion unless they show a consistent child-welfare disadvantage.  
4. When applicants meet the same protective standards and no such disadvantage is demonstrated, denying them solely because they are same-sex couples substitutes prejudice or speculation for child-centered assessment.

## Residual

The unresolved part is case-specific: the child’s history, sibling relationships, placement needs, and the applicants’ actual capabilities. General evidence cannot decide every individual adoption.

## Working answer

Yes, same-sex couples should be allowed to adopt, while being evaluated by the same rigorous, child-centered standards as different-sex couples. Equality establishes who may fairly be considered; child welfare determines which particular applicants are suitable.
