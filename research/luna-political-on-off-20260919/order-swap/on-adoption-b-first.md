## Frame B — Complementary-family ethics

This frame starts with a view of family rather than with administrative eligibility. It defines marriage and parenthood through the complementary roles of men and women; from that definition, it treats a mother-father household as the normative model for raising children; from that model, it gives public institutions reason to prefer such placements when all other factors are equal; and from that preference, it permits restrictions on same-sex adoption even when the couple is loving and capable. It may accept ordinary screening—stability, competence, safety, and commitment—as necessary, but it does not treat those criteria as the whole moral question.

## Frame A — Child-welfare placement

This frame starts with adoption’s institutional purpose: securing a safe, permanent, and nurturing home for a particular child. It evaluates applicants through screening, background checks, caregiving ability, stability, and the child’s individual needs; it then matches qualified adults to children; and it excludes applicants for demonstrated risks or inability to meet those needs, not for sexual orientation alone. Under this frame, same-sex couples should be eligible on the same terms as opposite-sex couples, while individual applicants and couples remain subject to the same child-welfare standards.

## Residual

The remaining questions are case-specific: the child’s wishes and needs, the quality of a particular home, jurisdictional law, and whether a placement will provide continuity, safety, and lasting commitment. Those questions require individual assessment rather than a categorical rule.

## Working answer

A same-sex couple should be allowed to adopt when they meet the same safety, stability, and caregiving standards applied to other applicants: the child-welfare frame makes demonstrated capacity the governing test, while the complementary-family frame presents a serious moral objection based on the value it assigns to male-female parenthood, not a demonstrated general incapacity of same-sex couples to raise children.
