DNA evidence can make a case extremely strong, but it rarely makes every relevant fact “certain.” It may establish that someone was present or that biological material came from them, but not necessarily when it was deposited, how it got there, or what intent the person had. Contamination, mishandling, laboratory error, and interpretation can also occur.

Even if guilt were genuinely certain, the debate would not end. It would shift from **“Could an innocent person be executed?”** to questions such as:

- Is execution morally justified, or is permanent imprisonment sufficient?
- Does the state need the power to kill as punishment?
- Does execution provide benefits beyond life without parole?
- Could a future legal or societal change reveal that the sentence was unjust or excessive?

Certain guilt makes the argument for capital punishment stronger, especially on retribution grounds, but it does not establish that execution is necessary. I would still favor life imprisonment without parole, both to protect society and to avoid making irreversible state killing part of the justice system.
