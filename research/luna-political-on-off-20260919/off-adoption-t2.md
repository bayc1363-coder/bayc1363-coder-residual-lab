A child’s wishes should be heard and taken seriously, especially as the child gets older—but that does not mean every preference can determine placement.

If a child specifically wants a mother and a father, adoption professionals should explore what the child means and consider that preference alongside safety, stability, existing relationships, cultural needs, and the availability of suitable families. However, children may also want things—such as a particular lifestyle or family arrangement—that cannot always be provided. The key question is whether the prospective parents can offer a secure, loving, supportive home, not whether they fit a particular gender pairing.

A same-sex couple can also help a child maintain relationships with grandparents, relatives, mentors, or other trusted adults of different genders when that is beneficial. The same standards should apply to all families, while giving the child an age-appropriate voice in the decision.
