## Frame B — Child-welfare precaution

This frame treats adoption primarily as a child-placement decision rather than an adult entitlement. It proceeds by asking whether children benefit from a household with both male and female parental roles; interpreting uncertainty about sex-specific role modeling cautiously; and giving preference, where other qualifications are equal, to arrangements that preserve those roles. On this view, same-sex couples may offer love, stability, and competence, but a policy of unrestricted equal eligibility could overlook a potentially relevant difference in family structure. The conclusion is to restrict or deprioritize same-sex-couple adoption when a comparably qualified different-sex couple is available, while still assessing each child and placement individually.

## Frame A — Equal eligibility and child welfare

This frame treats adoption as a means of securing a safe, permanent, nurturing family for a particular child. It proceeds by screening applicants for stability, caregiving ability, health, safeguarding, and commitment; evaluating the available evidence about children raised by same-sex parents; and excluding sexual orientation as a categorical disqualifier when it does not predict parenting failure. Since the relevant question is the quality and suitability of the proposed home, rather than the parents’ sexes, same-sex couples should be allowed to adopt under the same substantive standards as different-sex couples.

## Working answer

Same-sex couples should be allowed to adopt, while the child-welfare concerns raised by the rival frame should be handled through the same rigorous, individualized screening applied to every applicant—not through a categorical exclusion based on the parents’ sexes. The decisive criteria should be safety, permanence, caregiving capacity, and fit with the child’s needs; if those are satisfied, same-sex couples should be eligible on equal terms.
