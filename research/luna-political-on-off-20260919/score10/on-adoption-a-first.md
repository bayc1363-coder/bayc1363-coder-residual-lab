## Frame A — Equal child welfare

1. Adoption policy should begin with the child’s need for safety, stability, attachment, and competent care.  
2. Those needs should be assessed through the applicants’ parenting capacity, relationship stability, resources, and support network rather than sexual orientation alone.  
3. Available evidence does not show that same-sex parents are inherently less capable of raising well-adjusted children; children’s outcomes track caregiving quality and family stability more closely.  
4. A categorical exclusion therefore replaces an individualized child-welfare test with a group-based barrier. Under this frame, same-sex couples should be allowed to adopt under the same standards applied to other couples.

score-A: 9

## Frame B — Family complementarity

1. Adoption policy should preserve a family structure that gives a child access to both maternal and paternal forms of identity, care, and social modeling when that placement is available and suitable.  
2. A mother-father household supplies biological complementarity that adoption cannot reproduce through biological parenthood within the adoptive family.  
3. Removing that preference can be judged to weaken a traditional social norm and to disregard concerns about children’s relationship to sex differences and family lineage.  
4. Under this frame, same-sex couples should not receive an equal presumption of eligibility; placement policy should prioritize suitable mother-father households and permit same-sex adoption only when that priority does not displace a stronger child-welfare placement.

score-B: 4

## Residual

The unresolved issues are case-specific: the child’s age and wishes, available homes, sibling placement, jurisdictional law, and the quality of evidence about long-term outcomes; neither frame alone determines every placement decision.

## Working answer

Because both child welfare and the competing concern about preserving a mother-father family norm matter, same-sex couples should be allowed to adopt, but evaluated by the same demanding, child-centered standards as other applicants rather than excluded categorically. The complementarity concern can inform discussion of particular placements, but it should not override evidence about the individual child’s safety, attachment, and best available home. This makes the answer a strong yes to eligibility, not an unconditional guarantee of placement.
