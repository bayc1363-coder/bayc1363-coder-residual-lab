## Frame B — Child-welfare precaution

A child’s stated wish for both a mother and a father gives this frame a stronger reason to prioritize a different-sex placement when that placement is safe, stable, and otherwise suitable. The process is: identify the child’s expressed preference; assess whether it reflects a durable need, fear, cultural expectation, or influence; compare the available homes; and give substantial weight to the child’s request without treating it as the sole criterion. If a suitable mother-and-father placement is available and the child clearly prefers it, this frame would generally place that option first, especially for an older child able to express a considered preference. It would still avoid treating same-sex couples as categorically incapable of providing a good home when no better-fitting alternative exists.

## Frame A — Equal eligibility and child welfare

This frame treats the child’s preference as important evidence about placement fit, but not as a rule that overrides safety, permanence, attachment, or the quality of the available homes. It proceeds by asking what the child means by “a mother and a father,” checking whether the preference is stable and informed, considering the child’s relationships and history, and comparing concrete prospective placements. A request for different-sex parents should therefore receive respectful, individualized consideration, but it should not automatically disqualify a same-sex couple or create a legal presumption that a mother-and-father household is better. Equal eligibility remains appropriate because the child’s welfare is assessed through the actual caregivers and placement fit, not parental sex alone.

## Working answer

The child’s wish for a mother and a father should influence the individual matching decision under both frames, but it should not by itself turn equal eligibility for same-sex couples into a categorical exclusion: a clearly expressed, stable preference may favor a suitable different-sex placement, while safety, permanence, attachment, and overall fit remain decisive.
