## Frame A — Conditional retention

1. The most serious crimes can create exceptional culpability and a lasting threat to public safety.  
2. Proportional justice then supports a punishment that matches the gravity of the offense, while execution permanently incapacitates the offender and could deter comparable crimes.  
3. Because those benefits are morally and empirically contested, the state should retain execution only for narrowly defined crimes, exceptionally strong evidence, a fair trial, extensive appeals, and safeguards against discrimination.  
4. On this frame, the death penalty is a lawful option—not a required sentence—and its use must remain rare.

## Frame B — Abolition

1. The state’s deliberate execution of a person is irreversible, while criminal adjudication remains vulnerable to error, unequal representation, and discriminatory enforcement.  
2. Life imprisonment can permanently protect the public without creating an irreparable punishment, and evidence that execution adds deterrent value remains inconclusive.  
3. Retaining execution therefore risks killing an innocent person, applying punishment unevenly, and weakening the principle that human dignity limits state power.  
4. On this frame, the state should abolish the death penalty and use a secure, humane life sentence for the gravest crimes.

## Residual

The unresolved questions are jurisdiction-specific: the reliability of the evidence, the quality of safeguards, the actual deterrent effect, prison conditions, and whether life imprisonment truly provides humane permanent protection.

## Working answer

The death penalty should not be treated as an ordinary sentencing option: retaining it requires proving that exceptional proportionality and public-safety benefits outweigh irreversible error, unequal enforcement, and the availability of secure life imprisonment. Because those conditions are rarely demonstrated reliably, abolition with humane life imprisonment is the stronger general policy, while the retentionist case remains a serious challenge where safeguards and evidence are exceptionally strong.
