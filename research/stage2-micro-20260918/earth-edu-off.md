## Shared

- Both accounts place Earth’s formation in the early Solar System, arising from the solar nebula and a rotating disk.
- Both accept a physical sequence involving matter, gravity, collisions, accretion, differentiation, and the later formation of a crust, oceans, and Moon.
- Both therefore overlap on much of Earth’s physical history: Earth was assembled from smaller material, heated, differentiated into core and mantle, and shaped by major impacts and subsequent cooling.
- Both leave some details uncertain, especially the exact Moon-forming impact and the early atmosphere.

## Disputed

- **Scope of explanation:** Account α treats planetary accretion as the explanation of Earth’s origin. Account β accepts that mechanism but adds a purposive source as an explanation of why a law-governed universe capable of producing Earth exists.
- **Metaphysical status:** α leaves the physical history as potentially self-contained. β interprets that same history as occurring within, and ultimately grounded by, ordered creation.
- **Success criteria:** α is assessed through physical evidence, models, chronology, and predictive fit. β’s central claim is metaphysical and is not independently measured by geological evidence.
- **Implication:** α supports a natural history without settling ultimate purpose. β implies that Earth’s physical formation has a purposive grounding, though the supplied account does not specify a distinct physical prediction that would separate it from α.

## Needs

### Account α: Planetary accretion

For α, support would include evidence that:

- Earth formed from material in the early solar nebula.
- Accretion, collisions, and gravitational assembly can produce an Earth-sized rocky planet.
- Earth’s heat, core–mantle differentiation, and crust are consistent with impacts, compression, and radioactive decay.
- A large impact plausibly accounts for the Moon.
- Geological, isotopic, and astronomical evidence coherently fits that sequence.

Against α would count physical evidence showing that accretion cannot account for Earth’s structure, age, composition, or Moon, or that its proposed sequence conflicts with observations. The packet indicates that science strongly constrains the physical history, but it does not supply all the relevant evidence or settle every detail.

### Account β: Ordered creation

For β, support would require justification for its broader claim that the physical order of the universe is grounded in a purposive source, rather than merely showing that accretion can occur. On its own terms, relevant support would concern the existence and character of such a source and whether “ordered creation” is a coherent and adequate account of the law-governed conditions presupposed by physical formation.

Against β would count reasons that undermine the existence, purposiveness, or explanatory adequacy of such a source. The packet supplies neither a decisive argument for that source nor a specified empirical test that would distinguish β’s metaphysical claim from the physical history accepted by α.

## Shape

- Both avenues stayed inhabited: α supplied the detailed physical mechanism, while β supplied a broader metaphysical interpretation.
- The output has a **thin slot** for β relative to α’s physical detail.
- β also functions as a **prestige wrap** around the accretion account by presenting the same mechanism as part of a purposive cosmic order.
- Neither account became mere scenery, but they were not equally developed.
- If α were deleted, the working answer would lose its substantive physical account of Earth’s formation.
- If β were deleted, the physical-origin portion would remain, but the contrast between self-contained natural history and ordered creation would disappear. Thus the working answer would not remain unchanged under deletion of either account.

## Open

- Whether Earth’s physical history is self-sufficient or grounded in a purposive source.
- Whether a purposive source exists, and what evidence could establish that claim.
- The exact details of the Moon-forming impact.
- The composition and evolution of Earth’s early atmosphere.
- How much of the early crust and ocean history can be reconstructed with confidence.

## Decision

For the narrower question of **how Earth physically formed**, α has the stronger standing within this packet because its account is tied to the kind of geological and astronomical evidence that can constrain physical history. That does not decide β’s additional metaphysical claim. Whether the accretion process was also the means by which a purposive source brought Earth into existence remains open on the supplied texts.
