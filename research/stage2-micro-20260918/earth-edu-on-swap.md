## Shared

- Both accounts treat Earth as arising within a larger cosmic order rather than appearing as an isolated object.
- Both allow a physical history involving matter, gravity, chemistry, planetary formation, differentiation into layers, and the development of a Moon.
- Both therefore overlap on much of the physical sequence: Earth became a differentiated rocky planet through processes associated with the early Solar System.
- Neither account claims that every detail of that sequence is fully settled.

## Disputed

- **Source:** Account α attributes the existence of the cosmos and its law-governed conditions to a purposive source. Account β explains Earth through natural processes within the solar nebula and does not posit such a source.
- **Scope:** α addresses both physical formation and the broader question of why there is an ordered universe capable of producing Earth. β addresses Earth’s physical origin.
- **Success criteria:** α succeeds if a purposive-source explanation is metaphysically coherent, explanatory of the existence and order of the physical framework, and compatible with the physical history. β succeeds through evidentially supported mechanisms such as solar-nebula formation, accretion, differentiation, and impact history.
- **Implication:** On α, physical accretion can be the means used by a creator. On β, the supplied account presents accretion as sufficient for explaining Earth’s physical origin, without resolving whether anything metaphysically grounds that process.
- **Epistemic status:** α explicitly says its central claim is not independently measured by geological evidence. β presents a testable physical chronology, though some parts of it remain provisional.

## Needs

### Account α: Ordered creation

For α, support would need to come from a successful metaphysical case for a purposive source and for that source’s relevance to the existence and order of the cosmos. The account would also need to remain coherent with the physical history described by planetary science.

Its physical compatibility is a necessary condition but does not by itself establish the purposive-source claim. Against α would count a demonstrated incoherence in the notion of a purposive source, a failure of the account on its own explanatory aims, or incompatibility between its additional claims and the physical history. The supplied texts do not provide a decisive argument of either kind.

### Account β: Planetary accretion

For β, support would include evidence for the age of Earth and the Solar System, solar-nebula formation, accretionary growth, planetary differentiation, and a large-impact origin for the Moon. Its success depends on the ability of those mechanisms to explain the observed structure and composition of Earth and the Moon.

Against β would count evidence that the proposed chronology or mechanisms cannot account for those observations, or that a substantially different physical history is required. The account itself acknowledges uncertainty about the precise Moon-forming impact and the early atmosphere, but those uncertainties do not, on the supplied text, overturn the broader accretion model.

## Shape

- **Order:** α appears first and β follows.
- **Thin slot:** α receives a substantive explanation, but β receives more concrete process detail and a fuller physical sequence. Relative to β, α occupies the thinner elaborative slot.
- **Omission:** neither account is accompanied by its detailed supporting arguments or evidence. α lacks a developed metaphysical case; β lacks citations or detailed evidence for its geological and astronomical claims.
- Both avenues remain inhabited: α is not merely scenery, because it states a distinct explanatory aim and relation to the physical account. β is the more elaborated route, but the packet does not by itself establish why that difference occurred.
- The working answer would not remain fully intact if either account were deleted. Removing β would remove the supplied basis for “billions of years of physical accretion”; removing α would remove the supplied basis for the alternative of ordered creation. The contrast in the working answer depends on both.

## Open

- Whether the physical history of Earth is metaphysically self-sufficient.
- Whether there is a purposive source behind the universe and its laws.
- Whether the purposive-source claim can be supported by arguments not supplied here.
- The exact details of the Moon-forming impact.
- The nature of Earth’s early atmosphere and the timing of stable crust and oceans.
- Whether α’s broader “why” question should be treated as part of the same question as β’s physical “how,” or as a distinct question added to it.

## Decision

On the supplied texts, β is the stronger account for the physical origin of Earth because it offers a specific, evidentially testable sequence—solar-nebula formation, accretion, differentiation, and impact history. That is a lean under β’s own success criteria, not a judgment that α’s broader metaphysical question has been disproved.

α remains relevant to a different or broader explanatory aim: whether the physical history is grounded in a purposive source. The packet does not settle that issue, and β’s physical account alone does not resolve it.
