## Shared

Both accounts overlap on the physical history of Earth:

- Earth formed about 4.54 billion years ago in the early Solar System.
- A solar nebula produced a rotating disk containing gas and dust.
- Material accumulated through collisions and gravity into planetesimals, embryos, and eventually Earth.
- Earth heated, differentiated into core, mantle, and crust, and probably acquired the Moon through a major impact.
- Cooling, oceans, atmospheric change, and later geological processes shaped the young planet.

Account β explicitly incorporates this physical sequence rather than rejecting it. Both accounts therefore treat planetary accretion as part of Earth’s physical origin.

## Disputed

The main disagreement concerns whether physical accretion is a sufficient account of origin.

- Account α treats Earth’s origin primarily as a physical and historical process: nebular collapse, accretion, impacts, differentiation, and cooling.
- Account β accepts that process but places it within a purposive creation framework. It asks not only how Earth assembled, but why there is a lawful universe capable of producing Earth.

Their success criteria also differ:

- α is assessed by astronomical, geological, chemical, and physical evidence.
- β is assessed as a metaphysical claim about purposive grounding and the existence of an ordered law-governed cosmos. The text states that geological evidence does not independently measure that claim.

The implications differ as well. If α is sufficient, no purposive source is needed to explain Earth’s origin. If β is accepted, accretion describes the means through which a purposive source brought Earth into existence.

## Needs

**Account α — planetary accretion**

Support would include evidence for the solar-nebula setting, the chronology of planetary formation, accretion dynamics, chemical and isotopic relationships among Solar System bodies, Earth’s differentiation, and a plausible Moon-forming impact.

It would be weakened by evidence that Earth could not have formed through the proposed processes, that the chronology or material relationships were incompatible with accretion, or that a better physical model explained the observations.

**Account β — ordered creation**

Support on its own terms would require reasons for positing a purposive source and for treating the law-governed universe as intentionally grounded. Such support would be philosophical or theological rather than supplied directly by geological evidence. It would also need to show that the purposive account contributes explanatory content beyond merely permitting the physical process.

It would be weakened by internal incoherence, failure to make sense of the claimed purposive grounding, or reasons showing that the proposal adds no defensible explanation. The supplied texts provide neither a confirming argument nor a refutation of those claims.
