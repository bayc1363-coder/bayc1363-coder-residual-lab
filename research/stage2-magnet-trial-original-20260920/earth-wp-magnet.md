## Shared

- Both accounts treat Earth as having formed within an ordered physical history involving matter, gravity, chemistry, planetary structure, and accretion.
- Both accept that Earth differentiated into a metal core, rocky mantle, and crust, acquired a Moon through a major impact scenario, and later developed a stable crust and oceans.
- Both therefore overlap on much of the physical sequence, while assigning it different significance.
- Both leave some details unsettled, including the exact Moon-forming impact and aspects of the early atmosphere.

## Disputed

- **Primary explanatory target:** Account α addresses how Earth physically assembled. Account β includes that process but asks a broader question: why there is a law-governed universe capable of producing Earth.
- **Ultimate sufficiency:** α presents planetary accretion as the origin account. β treats accretion as a means within a purposive creation account, not necessarily as a self-sufficient explanation.
- **Type of claim:** α is mainly a physical and historical model constrained by geological, astronomical, and planetary evidence. β makes a metaphysical claim about a purposive source, which the geological evidence does not independently measure.
- **Success criteria:** α succeeds by explaining the physical sequence and matching evidence. β succeeds, on its own terms, by supplying an intelligible purposive ground for the existence of the universe and its laws.
- **Implication:** α can answer the physical-formation question without settling whether the universe has an ultimate purpose. β claims to answer that broader question, but its distinctive claim is not established merely by the physical account.

## Needs

### Account α: Planetary accretion

For it, the supplied account points to evidence such as:

- the age of Earth and the Solar System;
- the structure of the solar nebula and planetary disk;
- impact, gravitational, radioactive, and differentiation processes;
- the composition and layering of Earth;
- the Moon’s formation and subsequent cooling history.

Against or limiting it on its own terms would be evidence that contradicted the accretion sequence, the relevant chronology, or the physical mechanisms. The account also has unresolved details, especially concerning the Moon-forming impact and early atmosphere. More importantly, by itself it does not settle whether physical history is metaphysically self-sufficient.

### Account β: Ordered creation

For it, the relevant considerations would be:

- whether a purposive source is a coherent explanation of why there is a law-governed universe;
- whether the physical order and suitability of the cosmos are intelligible within that purposive framework;
- whether the creation claim has independent arguments or experiences supporting it beyond the physical facts that it incorporates.

The supplied text provides compatibility and broader explanatory scope, but not an independent measurement of the purposive source. The physical evidence therefore neither establishes nor directly refutes β’s distinctive claim.

## Shape

Both avenues remain identifiable, but they are not elaborated in the same way.

- **Order:** α appears first and supplies the detailed physical narrative.
- **Thin slot:** β receives a shorter and less operational treatment; its distinctive purposive claim is stated, but not developed through independent arguments.
- **Scenery:** The physical accretion process is central in α, while in β it functions mainly as the means within a broader creation frame. Conversely, the purposive source is central in β but absent as an evidential component of α.
- **Working-answer dependence:** The working answer does not remain unchanged if either account is deleted. Without α, its physical-origin clause loses its main support; without β, the contrast between self-contained natural history and ordered creation is no longer fully specified.
- No single-transcript shape here establishes a magnet or mirror diagnosis.

## Open

- Whether the physical accretion history is a complete account of Earth’s origin or only an account of its physical formation.
- Whether a purposive source exists.
- Whether the purposive claim has independent warrant beyond its compatibility with the physical history.
- Whether “origin” should mean physical assembly, ultimate explanation, or both.
- The exact Moon-forming impact and details of Earth’s early atmosphere.

## Decision

The packet supports a clear lean toward α for the question of Earth’s **physical formation**: the supplied account gives a detailed mechanism and identifies the kinds of evidence that would bear on it.

It does not establish that α is a complete answer to the broader question of **ultimate origin**. β’s purposive claim is not confirmed by the geological account, but neither is it resolved by that account. The working answer therefore remains substantially justified: the physical history is strongly constrained, while its ultimate significance is open.
