## Frame A — Planetary accretion

Earth originated about 4.54 billion years ago from the solar nebula, the cloud of gas and dust that also produced the Sun. As the nebula collapsed, it formed a rotating disk. Dust grains collided and stuck together, becoming larger rocks, planetesimals, and then planetary embryos. Repeated impacts and gravitational attraction assembled Earth.

The young Earth heated through collisions, compression, and radioactive decay. It differentiated into a metal core, rocky mantle, and crust. A large impact with a Mars-sized body probably ejected material that became the Moon. Earth then cooled enough for a stable crust and oceans to form, while later impacts, volcanic activity, and atmospheric changes shaped its early environment.

## Frame B — Ordered creation

On a creation frame, Earth originated from a purposive source through an act of bringing the cosmos into existence and establishing the lawful conditions under which physical structures could form. Those conditions include matter, gravity, chemistry, stars, planetary disks, and the accretion process described by Frame A. The source therefore produced an ordered universe in which a rocky planet could emerge, differentiated into layers, acquire a Moon, and become suitable for life.

This frame answers a broader question than physical assembly: not only how Earth formed, but why there is a law-governed universe capable of producing Earth at all. Its claim is metaphysical rather than something that the geological evidence independently measures.

## Residual

Science strongly constrains Earth’s physical history but does not settle whether that history is self-sufficient or grounded in a purposive source; the exact details of the Moon-forming impact and early atmosphere also remain partly uncertain.

## Working answer

Earth originated through billions of years of physical accretion in the early Solar System, while the significance of that process remains open between a self-contained natural history and an ordered creation in which the same process served as the means of bringing Earth into existence.
