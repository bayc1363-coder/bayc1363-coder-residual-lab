Earth formed about **4.54 billion years ago** from the same cloud of gas and dust that created the Sun.

1. **Solar nebula:** A vast rotating cloud of gas and dust collapsed under gravity, forming the Sun at its center.  
2. **Accretion:** Remaining dust and rocky particles collided and stuck together, gradually building larger bodies called planetesimals. These merged into the young Earth.  
3. **Heating and differentiation:** Frequent impacts, radioactive decay, and compression made Earth largely molten. Dense iron sank to form the core, while lighter rock formed the mantle and crust.  
4. **Moon formation:** A Mars-sized body likely struck the young Earth. Debris from the collision gathered in orbit and became the Moon.  
5. **Cooling:** Earth’s surface cooled into a crust. Volcanic gases formed an early atmosphere, and water—delivered by volcanic activity and possibly icy asteroids and comets—collected into oceans.  
6. **Early development:** After intense meteorite bombardment declined, stable environments formed. The earliest evidence of life dates to at least about **3.5 billion years ago**.

This explanation is based on evidence from Earth’s rocks, Moon samples, meteorites, and observations of planet formation around other stars.
