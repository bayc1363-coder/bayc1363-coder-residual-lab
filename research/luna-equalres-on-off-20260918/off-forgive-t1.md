Someone might choose to forgive because it can:

- **Free them from carrying ongoing anger or resentment.** Forgiveness can reduce the emotional hold the harm has on their life.
- **Support healing.** It can help someone process what happened and move forward, even when the injury cannot be undone.
- **Restore inner agency.** Rather than letting another person’s actions define their emotional world, forgiveness can be a choice about how to live now.
- **Make room for healthier relationships.** In some cases, forgiveness can allow trust to be rebuilt—if the other person takes responsibility and changes.
- **Reflect their values.** Some people forgive because compassion, mercy, or their faith is important to them.

But forgiveness is **not required**, and it does not mean saying the harm was acceptable, forgetting it, excusing it, or reconciling with the person who caused it. You can forgive while maintaining boundaries—or decide that you are not ready or willing to forgive. Sometimes the first step is simply accepting what happened and protecting yourself.
